## The brief
A fictional chamber music festival has a season to announce across its website and newsletter. A small team needs a publishing process that keeps the facts consistent and makes late changes manageable.

## Start with a shared concert record
For each event, confirm the title, date, time, venue, performers, repertoire, ticket URL, access information, approver, and publication date. Track each record as Draft, In review, Approved, Published, or Updated.

## A sample publishing sequence
- **Three weeks before announcement:** collect approved program details, artist biographies, photographs, and ticket links.
- **Two weeks before:** draft concert pages and newsletter copy from the same event records.
- **One week before:** collect consolidated feedback and verify repertoire, artist names, dates, and credits.
- **Before publication:** check mobile layouts, heading order, keyboard access, ticket destinations, and image alternatives.
- **Announcement day:** publish and verify the concert pages before sending the newsletter.

## An event page with a practical job
Lead with the concert title, date, time, venue, and ticket action. Follow with a brief program introduction, repertoire, performers, and venue access information. Label the ticket link with enough context to identify the event.

## Handle changes deliberately
If an artist or program changes, update the shared record first, secure approval, and revise every affected page. Decide whether ticket holders also need a direct message. Record what changed and when it was checked.

## After the concert
Remove expired ticket prompts, retain a useful program archive, and review links before the next season. Ongoing support can include these updates alongside new concert pages and announcements.

## How I would evaluate it
Check whether visitors can find event details and reach the correct ticket page. Review publishing errors and last-minute corrections with the team to improve the next announcement cycle. No ticket sales or audience growth results are claimed.
