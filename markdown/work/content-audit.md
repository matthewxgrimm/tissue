## The brief
A fictional violinist performs with an orchestra, teaches privately, and plays chamber music. Biography versions, recordings, and lesson details are scattered across messages and documents. The goal is one website the musician can confidently share.

## Give each visitor a clear path
- **Presenters:** a short biography, selected recordings with repertoire and performer credits, and a booking contact.
- **Prospective students and parents:** teaching information, location or online options, and a lesson inquiry route.
- **Concert organizers:** approved biography versions, a downloadable headshot with photographer credit, and press contact details.

## Proposed page structure
The home page introduces the musician and leads to Listen, Performances, Lessons, and Press. The biography has its own page so the opening introduction stays concise. A short navigation makes all sections easy to reach on a phone.

## Sample teaching copy
“Interested in violin lessons? Tell me about your playing experience and what you’d like to work on. Get in touch to ask about availability.”

Before publishing, confirm the actual teaching offer, studio policies, contact route, and any information intended for parents. Avoid promises about availability or outcomes that the musician has not approved.

## Materials and handoff
Gather an approved short and full biography, two selected recordings, an authorized headshot, public performance dates, and the preferred contact details. Include recording titles, composer names, performer credits, photo permissions, and a review date.

## What success would look like
Ask a presenter to find a recording and a parent to find the lesson inquiry route. Check whether each can complete the task without needing extra instructions. This concept proposes the structure; no working musician site or measured results are claimed.
